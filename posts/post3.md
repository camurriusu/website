# Abstract
This one is only for the music enjoyers. Actually, that's a lie, the people who enjoy music the most don't even think about this stuff. So this is for the people who instead like audio.  
  
Picture this, you just bought a new stereo and lied about the price to your wife. Goes without saying you're a middle aged man. You turn it on - ram a CD inside and listen to those nostalgic tunes. There's a tiny doubt growing in your aged mind as you lean back while being mindful to keep your ears level with the tweeters. The music sounds... weird. You put your old headphones back on. Sounds better. Your eyes start to feel a bit moist. You can't cry - the price was worth it, anyone would pay that much for that pure analogue sound. Is that really muddy bass part of the sound signature of your 10k$ analogue amp made of military grade capacitors?  

After hours of research (while hiding your growing frustration from your significant other), you find a whole new chapter in your audiophile journey. The art of room acoustics. You realise you put your stereo in a square room with tile floor and windows around it. But you can't put it anywhere else! You're forced to spend thousands on treatment so you can actually hear what your state of the art overpriced speakers can offer.  
  
That was my interpretation on what the most eventful day is to D4 dad.  
  
# Room size
Easy - small room bad, square room bad, big rectangle room good. Kind of useless information for most people, except for those who happen to have loads of different shaped rooms that happen to be free.  
  
If you want to know why the above is true, it's simple. Small rooms and square rooms have got the worst room modes. From physics, maybe you remember what a standing wave is - a wave with stationary nodes and moving antinodes. As the wave gets deflected back by your wall, intereference makes the amplitude even more exaggerated until the energy of the wave is lost by bouncing back and forth in your room.  
  
Try blaring the frequency you get from diving 340 by the distance between the wall behind you and your speakers, and note how much louder it sounds than normal. Touch the walls and windows - they're shaking.  
  
There's obviously way more standing waves going on in the room, but the worst one will be the first harmonic. We only like standing waves in strings and pipes, not rooms. It's also not that simple - there are different directions (especially for bass frequencies) that the waves can propagate around and hit walls at different angles, causing more unique standing waves.  
  
That's why Mr D4 was experiencing a muddy, overwhelming bass. Whenever he was playing Fleetwood Mac Dreams, that repetitive bass tone was shaking his poor eardrums and drowning out all the other frequencies he paid for.  
  
A square room is adds more problems because you'll get a lot of similar standing waves in all directions, creating huge interferences and zones of steep peaks and low nulls.  
A smaller room is worse because the standing wave's first harmonic will be at a higher and more commonly played frequency. Concert halls and the likes have room modes that are low enough that they don't matter.
  
# The Solution
## Absorbers
Bass traps is all most people will need, but a lot of them. Yeah, Mr D4's wife won't be happy when the living room looks like a solitary confinement cell with all that padding.  
  
Absorbers are just thick pieces of foam or whatever material that absorb waves and convert them to heat (not enough to save you gas money). The thicker the better. Corners, walls, ceiling, floor, you name it.

## Diffusers
Sometimes too many absorbers will make you feel dead - your music listening I mean. Diffusers add a little sparkle and don't really work in small rooms as they need space to "diffuse" i.e. randomly deflect waves everywhere. This helps keeping those high brilliant frequencies from getting eaten by the nasty absorbers.  
  
You can find hybrid panels that combine both diffusers and absorber by just stacking them on top of each other.  
  
# Conclusion
If you're rich, go ahead. If not, save money for a mortgage because those absorbers won't feel very comfy when you'll live in Grafton Street. Truth is, your stereo imaging will never be perfect and you can't get rid of all room modes without your room looking like a bunker. Wear headphones if you can't stand it.
